# Tikt

**Bitter is Beautiful.**

Tikt is a canned Americano cocktail — soda water, Campari, sweet vermouth — at 5% ABV. The name comes from Sanskrit, meaning "bitter." Handcrafted in Chicago.

This repo is the marketing landing page at [arv.in/tikt](https://arv.in/tikt), built to gauge market interest via email waitlist signup.

## Stack

Single `index.html` with embedded CSS and vanilla JS. No build tools, no frameworks. Hosted on GitHub Pages.

- **Fonts**: Cormorant Garamond + DM Sans (Google Fonts)
- **Email capture**: [Formspree](https://formspree.io)
- **Analytics**: [GoatCounter](https://goatcounter.com)

## Brand Guide

### Positioning

Tikt is the sophisticated alternative to sweet seltzers. Where White Claw and its peers lean sweet and simple, Tikt leans bitter, complex, and worldly. It brings aperitivo culture — the ritual of a pre-dinner bitter — into the convenience of a can. The reference point is a sun-drenched Italian piazza, not a pool party.

### Name & Origin

**Tikt** (तिक्त) — bitter, in Sanskrit. Short, sharp, memorable. It nods to the ancient roots of bitter botanicals while sounding modern and minimal.

### Tagline

> Bitter is Beautiful.

Secondary lines:
- "A savory seltzer"
- "Bitter digestif"
- "The aperitivo hour, canned"

### Voice & Tone

**Warm, confident, unhurried.** Tikt speaks like someone who knows what they like and doesn't need to explain it. Never shouty, never salesy, never trying too hard.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Speak with quiet confidence | Shout or use exclamation marks |
| Use short, declarative sentences | Over-explain or hedge |
| Reference ritual, craft, tradition | Sound clinical or technical |
| Let the product speak for itself | Hard-sell or create urgency |
| Be inclusive and inviting | Be exclusive or pretentious |

**Example copy**:
- "Not everything worth drinking is sweet."
- "Three ingredients. Centuries of tradition."
- "Sip it straight from the can. No judgment."

**Words we use**: bitter, savory, craft, ritual, aperitivo, botanical, tradition, warmth, golden hour
**Words we avoid**: refreshing (too generic), party, crush, slam, buzz, low-cal, guilt-free

The palette is pulled directly from the can design: terracotta and copper over charcoal, with intricate mandala patterns inspired by South Asian art. Warm, earthy, never neon.

### Typography

- **Cormorant Garamond** — Headlines and display text. A high-contrast serif that evokes Italian editorial design and aperitivo culture. Use weights 300 (display), 400 (headings), and 500 (wordmark).
- **DM Sans** — Body, UI, and navigation. A clean geometric sans-serif for readability. Use weights 400 (body) and 500 (labels, buttons, uppercase text).

Headlines are large and light (weight 300). Body text is small and steady (weight 400). The contrast between ornate serif and clean sans-serif mirrors the product itself — tradition meets modern convenience.

### Photography Direction

- **Lighting**: Golden hour, warm natural light. Never flash, never studio-white.
- **Settings**: Outdoor tables, Mediterranean and park scenes, weathered wood, slate surfaces.
- **Props**: Citrus (orange peel, grapefruit), olives, star anise, dried botanicals. Real food, not styled.
- **People**: Diverse, relaxed, mid-conversation. Never posed or looking at camera.
- **Mood**: The last hour of sunlight on a long afternoon. Unhurried.

### Can Design

The can features an intricate mandala pattern in dark ink over a terracotta/copper base, with a central diamond label reading "Bitter digestif / TIKT / A savory seltzer." The design draws from South Asian decorative art, connecting the Sanskrit name to a visual tradition of dense, beautiful pattern work. It should look like something you'd want to keep on a shelf.
